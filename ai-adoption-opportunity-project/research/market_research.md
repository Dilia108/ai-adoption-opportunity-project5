# Market Research: AI Adoption in Logistics & Freight Forwarding

**Project:** AI Adoption Opportunity — Document Intelligence
**Client:** CEO Olaf Müller, Hamburg-based logistics and transportation company (500 employees, 16 locations, 8 countries)


---

## 1. Industry Overview

The global freight forwarding market is valued at USD 178 billion in 2025 and projected to reach
USD 250 billion by 2034, driven by a CAGR of 3.8%. AI and machine learning are already improving
route optimisation, predictive analytics, and automated document processing across the sector.
*(Source: GlobeNewswire / ResearchAndMarkets, June 2025 —
https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/06/24/3104015/28124/en/Freight-Forwarding-Industry-Outlook-Report-2025-2034-AI-and-Blockchain-Revolutionizing-the-249-Bn-Market.html)*

The AI-in-supply-chain market specifically is valued at USD 9.94 billion in 2025 and projected to
reach USD 236 billion by 2035 — a 30%+ CAGR.
*(Source: Precedence Research via Open Sky Group, April 2026 —
https://openskygroup.com/supply-chain-ai-statistics/)*

## 2. AI Adoption Signals

### 2.1 Industry-wide adoption rates

- **70%** of logistics and transportation companies now report adopting AI solutions — up 17% year-on-year.
  Top gains: fleet planning (36%), route optimisation (35%), operational efficiency (34%).
  *(Source: Penske Transportation Leaders Survey 2025 —
  https://www.truckinginfo.com/10241279/ai-in-logistics-penske-survey-uncovers-surging-adoption-rising-concerns)*

- **72%** of logistics employees adopted AI tools in 2024 — the highest rate across all industries.
  *(Source: ActivTrak 2025 via Open Sky Group —
  https://openskygroup.com/supply-chain-ai-statistics/)*

- **94%** of supply chain companies plan to use AI or generative AI for decision support within two years.
  *(Source: ABI Research 2025 via Open Sky Group —
  https://openskygroup.com/supply-chain-ai-statistics/)*

- **85%** of executives plan to increase AI spending in 2026, with one in five expecting a 20%+ increase.
  *(Source: Supply Chain Brain 2025 via Open Sky Group —
  https://openskygroup.com/supply-chain-ai-statistics/)*

### 2.2 Mid-market gap

Only **28%** of mid-sized logistics providers (USD 10M–100M annual revenue) have implemented
comprehensive AI solutions, compared to 73% of large enterprises. Cloud-based, consumption-priced
AI tools are beginning to close this gap.
*(Source: DocShipper 2025 —
https://docshipper.com/logistics/ai-changing-logistics-supply-chain-2025/)*

### 2.3 Generative AI signal

**29%** of supply chain executives plan to fundamentally reinvent their supply chain management
using generative AI — specifically in forecasting, capacity planning, and pricing optimisation.
*(Source: Accenture study via Maersk, December 2024 —
https://www.maersk.com/insights/digitalisation/2024/12/18/what-to-expect-in-2025-supply-chain-tech-trends)*

### 2.4 Company-specific adoption signals — Müller's company

While industry-wide adoption rates establish the broader context, the following operational characteristics of Müller's company are themselves classic indicators of AI readiness and fit — independently of the industry trend. Each signal maps directly to a condition where Document Intelligence delivers measurable value.

| Signal | Present | Why it matters for AI adoption |
|---|---|---|
| Existing TMS usage | ✓ | Confirms data infrastructure and operational discipline; Document Intelligence integrates as an additional layer, not a replacement |
| International operations (multi-country, multi-customs regime) | ✓ | 8 countries × multiple customs regimes = high daily document volume and regulatory complexity — the core conditions for automation ROI |
| E-commerce customers | ✓ | E-commerce logistics generates high-frequency, standardised document flows — well-suited to AI extraction and pre-population |
| Contract logistics | ✓ | Long-term client agreements require consistent, auditable documentation — AI-generated records directly support compliance and client reporting obligations |
| Hazardous goods logistics | ✓ | Hazmat shipments require precise, error-free documentation across multiple regulatory frameworks — the cost of a documentation error is disproportionately high, making accuracy automation especially valuable |

**Reading the signals:** All five indicators are present. This is not a company that needs to build readiness for AI — the operational environment that makes Document Intelligence valuable already exists. The question is not whether to adopt, but where to start.

## 6. Regulatory Signals

### EU Customs Reform (2024–2025)
The most ambitious reform of EU customs rules since 1968 introduces the EU Customs Data Hub.
AI will be used by customs authorities to analyse data and predict problems before goods begin their
journey. A new "trust and check trader" tier rewards companies with clean, accurate, auditable customs
data with faster clearance and fewer inspections.
*(Source: European Commission —
https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/customs/eu-customs-reform_en)*

### EU AI Act
Full enforcement from 2 August 2026. Document Intelligence for logistics is classified as
**minimal risk** — no conformity assessment required. 53.8% of German enterprises had implemented
no concrete compliance measures as of November 2025.
*(Source: Deloitte November 2025 via Sphere Partners —
https://www.sphereinc.com/blogs/eu-ai-act-risk-classification-guide)*

### CSRD
Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive expands to mid-size companies from 2026–2027.
Shipment data extracted for customs purposes (mode, distance, weight, carrier) directly feeds
CSRD emissions reporting — eliminating a separate data collection process.
