Three AI opportunities were evaluated for Müller's company. All three are technically feasible and financially material. The question is sequencing: which to pursue first, and in what order.

### Opportunity A — Operational Copilot for Planners

**What it does:** Trains on historical TMS data (shipment volumes, routes, timing, client orders)
to predict future demand, optimise load planning, and reduce empty kilometres.

**Evidence of impact:**
- AI-powered forecasting reduces forecast errors by 20–50% and lowers logistics costs by up to 15%
  *(Source: McKinsey 2025)*
- A 2025 peer-reviewed study found AI reduced forecast error by up to 50%, decreased logistics
  costs by 31.5%, and reduced empty kilometres by 15%
  *(Source: Journal of Business & Entrepreneurial Studies 2025)*
- DB Schenker and Maersk both report this as their primary AI investment area

**Implementation profile:**
- Requires: 2+ years of clean, structured TMS history
- Timeline to ROI: 6–18 months
- Cost: medium–high
- Risk: data quality dependency — if historical data is incomplete or inconsistent,
  model accuracy suffers significantly

**What it does:** Automatically reads incoming logistics documents (invoices, customs declarations,

**Evidence of impact:**

### 3.2 Verdict — why Document Intelligence is the stronger first move

**1. No data dependency.**
The Operational Copilot and Shipment Visibility opportunities both require years of clean,
structured TMS and shipment history. Müller's data quality across 16 locations in 8 countries —
including African corridors where data infrastructure varies — cannot be assumed to be clean and
complete. Document Intelligence starts with documents that already exist today.

**2. Fastest payback in logistics AI.**
60–90 days to ROI is the fastest documented payback of any AI use case in this sector. At 4–7%
net margins, a 2–3% cost reduction translates to a 30–75% margin improvement. That is a
board-level number.

**3. Directly addresses the company's cross-border exposure.**
A company operating across 8 countries processes customs declarations, freight invoices, and
trade documents in multiple languages and regulatory formats every day. That is exactly the
high-frequency, error-prone environment where Document Intelligence delivers the most value.

**4. Kuehne+Nagel has proven the revenue case.**
Kuehne+Nagel reported customs AI as a standout growth driver in both 2024 and 2025 results.
This is not a future prediction — it is a documented competitive outcome from a structurally
similar operator.

**5. Regulatory tailwind, not headwind.**
The EU Customs Reform and the new EU Customs Data Hub actively reward operators with clean,
auditable customs records. Document Intelligence builds exactly that track record — creating
a regulatory advantage the other two opportunities do not.

**6. Document Intelligence enables all three opportunities in sequence.**
Once Document Intelligence is running, the structured, clean data it generates — shipment
records, invoice data, customs clearance times — becomes the data foundation that the
Operational Copilot requires. And once the Copilot is generating consolidated, high-quality
operational data, Shipment Visibility & Risk Prediction becomes the natural third move.
The three opportunities are sequential, not competing.

## 5. Use Case Selected

**AI-Powered Document Intelligence for Customs & Invoice Processing**

Automated extraction, validation, and routing of data from logistics documents — customs
declarations, freight invoices, bills of lading, and proof-of-delivery documents — using
Optical Character Recognition (OCR), Natural Language Processing (NLP), and machine learning.

## 6. Problem Statement

Every international shipment Müller's company handles requires a set of trade documents:

| Document | Purpose | Manual pain point |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial invoice | Declares shipment value and items for customs | Multi-line data entry per shipment; pricing errors cause duty miscalculations |
| Bill of lading (B/L) | Legal contract between shipper and carrier | Shipper, consignee, cargo, route — all re-keyed into TMS manually |
| Customs declaration | Filed with authorities at each border | Hours per declaration; one wrong HS code = USD 10,000 fine and container held |
| Packing list | Details quantities, weights, dimensions | Must match invoice and B/L exactly — discrepancies cause inspection delays |
| Certificate of origin | Proves where goods were manufactured | Required for preferential tariff treatment; sourced and attached manually |
| Proof of delivery (POD) | Confirms receipt at destination | Scanned or faxed; manually validated and filed |

With 16 locations across 8 countries processing shipments daily, the cumulative manual effort
and error exposure is significant. A single mistyped Harmonised System (HS) code can result in
a USD 10,000 fine and halt a container worth millions at port.
*(Source: DocStreams.ai, December 2025 —
https://docstreams.ai/blog/ai-in-maritime-and-logistics-the-document-processing-revolution-transforming-global-trade)*

## 7. How would the AI Solution Work

The process follows four stages, based on the model deployed by Customs Support Group across
14 European markets in 2025:

### Stage 1 — Document ingestion & classification
The system monitors incoming document streams — email attachments, FTP uploads, API feeds
from the TMS — and classifies each document by type. A commercial invoice routes to the invoice
extraction model. A packing list goes to the packing list model. A customs declaration is handled
separately.
*(Source: FreightMynd, January 2026 —
https://freightmynd.com/blog/customs-declaration-automation-ai/)*

### Stage 2 — AI extraction
OCR reads the document (including handwritten notes, low-resolution scans, and mobile phone
photos). NLP interprets context and extracts structured field values: shipper name, consignee,
HS code, declared value, weight, origin country, line items.

### Stage 3 — Confidence-scored validation
Each extracted field receives a confidence score:
- **Green** — high confidence, auto-approved for pre-population
- **Orange** — uncertain, flagged for human review
- **Red** — unreadable or missing, requires manual entry

AI can pre-populate **80–90%** of declaration fields automatically from commercial invoices,
packing lists, and certificates of origin.
*(Source: FreightMynd — https://freightmynd.com/blog/customs-declaration-automation-ai/)*

### Stage 4 — Human review and submission
A licensed customs declarant reviews flagged fields, applies expert judgment on HS classification
and duty optimisation, and signs off the declaration before submission to authorities.
**The legal responsibility never transfers to the AI.**

Corrected documents feed back to the model — it learns from each correction and improves over time.

## 9. Justification — Why This Use Case

### 8.1 Speed of return
Document Intelligence delivers ROI in **60–90 days** — faster than any other AI use case in
logistics. It does not require historical data collection, model training from scratch, or process
redesign. Existing documents are the input.
*(Source: Docsumo IDP Market Report 2025 —
https://www.docsumo.com/blogs/intelligent-document-processing/intelligent-document-processing-market-report-2025)*

### 8.2 Low implementation risk
The system is **additive** to existing workflows — it does not replace the TMS, the customs broker,
or the ERP. It sits between document receipt and data entry, eliminating the manual re-keying step.
If the AI fails on a document, the existing manual process handles it. There is no single point of failure.

### 8.3 Regulatory alignment
The EU Customs Reform and the new EU Customs Data Hub reward operators with clean, auditable,
consistently accurate customs records. Document Intelligence directly builds that track record —
supporting future AEO status and eventual "trust and check trader" eligibility.
*(Source: EU Council — https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/policies/modernising-the-eu-customs-union/)*

### 8.4 Scalable across all 16 locations
The same model handles documents from Germany, France, Ghana, South Africa, Canada, and the USA.
DB Schenker's architecture — one model adaptable to each country's specific needs — demonstrates
this is achievable at Müller's scale.
*(Source: DB Schenker Insights — https://www.dbschenker.com/global/insights/blog/smart-networks-built-smarter-1788904)*

### 8.5 Financially material at this margin level
Mid-market 3PLs operate on 4–7% net margins. A 2–3% total cost reduction through AI translates
to a **30–75% margin improvement**.
*(Source: European Logistics Association via The Thinking Company —
https://thinking.inc/en/industry-service/logistics-ai-roi/)*
